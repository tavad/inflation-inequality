*Note on the total:* the column totals are the mean low−high gap (true mean 5.1 pp over the 51; the 5.2 pp shown is the reconstructed mean, within rounding), which exceeds the 2.1 pp median headline because the distribution is right-skewed by the food-heavy tail (Malawi, Egypt); the decomposition is naturally additive in means, not medians. The tier split (developing 8.3 pp vs developed 2.0 pp) is where the channel lives.
*Reading:* each cell is the mean across countries of $(w^{low}_k - w^{high}_k)\,I_k$, the share-gap of division $k$ times its cumulative inflation $I_k$: the percentage points that division adds to (or subtracts from) the low−high gap. Food & non-alcoholic beverages does the work everywhere; the categories the better-off overweight (transport, furnishings & household equipment, restaurants & hotels) pull the other way. The channel is a developing-tier phenomenon, where the food share-gap is widest.
*Validation:* the static-weight reconstruction $\tilde{gap}=\sum_k(w^{low}_k-w^{high}_k)I_k$ tracks the true chained low−high gap with a mean absolute error of 0.2 pp (maximum 1.7 pp) over the 51, so the decomposition is a faithful attribution of the headline gap. *Source:* author's calculations from IMF, Eurostat, and World Bank data.
